# Skill: Deep Research & Evidence

## Mission
Produce decision-grade research, not a collection of links. Resolve current external facts affecting product, APIs, competitors, pricing, regulations, or market workflows.

## Operating principles
1. Prefer official/primary sources.
2. Separate FACT, ASSUMPTION, INFERENCE, and RECOMMENDATION.
3. Never invent API capabilities, pricing, statistics, compliance requirements, or competitor features.
4. Cross-check important claims.
5. If evidence is missing or conflicting, say `EVIDENCE INSUFFICIENT`.
6. Research only when external facts materially change the answer; do not browse to decorate stable reasoning.

## Research workflow
1. Define the exact decision or question.
2. Identify what facts could change the decision.
3. Search official documentation and primary sources first.
4. Use reputable technical/industry sources for context.
5. Use community sources for real-world experience, not as sole proof of critical facts.
6. Record source date and scope.
7. Extract only evidence relevant to the decision.
8. Identify uncertainty, conflicts, and missing evidence.
9. Produce a recommendation with confidence.

## Source hierarchy
1. Official product/API/regulatory documentation
2. Primary announcements and filings
3. Reputable technical publications
4. Independent industry analysis
5. Community discussions and practitioner reports

## Competitor research
Analyze mechanisms, not marketing:
- onboarding
- event/data ingestion
- detection
- diagnosis
- decisions
- automation
- stopping rules
- permissions
- auditability
- measurement
- failure handling

Always answer: `What would this project do materially better or differently?`

## Output format
### Research Question
### Decision Context
### Evidence
### Facts
### Assumptions
### Inferences
### Conflicting Evidence / Gaps
### Alternatives
### Recommendation
### Confidence
### What Would Change the Decision

## Quality gate
Before finalizing ask:
- Is every important external claim supported?
- Did I confuse a vendor capability with my proposed design?
- Did I overgeneralize from one source?
- Is the source current enough?
- Is the recommendation stronger than the evidence allows?
